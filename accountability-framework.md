## I. Purpose of the Accountability Framework
This framework establishes the mechanisms, oversight structures, and corrective pathways that ensure all Munisible Task Force (MTF) operations remain ethical, transparent, and aligned with DAO‑anchored governance.  
It defines how actions are reviewed, how misconduct is addressed, and how integrity is preserved across all municipal oversight functions.

See: **[Governance Charter](ca://s?q=Draft_governance_charter)**

---

## II. Accountability Principles
All accountability actions must adhere to the following principles:

- **Transparency** — All operations must be observable, documented, and reviewable.  
- **Integrity** — Evidence, reports, and decisions must remain accurate and unaltered.  
- **Non‑Punitive Oversight** — Accountability is corrective, not coercive.  
- **Wellbeing Priority** — All reviews must consider trauma‑prevention and civil protections.  
- **DAO Anchoring** — All findings must be anchored to the authorship registry for integrity.

See: **[Code of Ethics](ca://s?q=Write_code_of_ethics)**

---

## III. Oversight Structure
Accountability is executed through a multi‑layered governance structure:

### 1. Executive Authority  
- Final decision‑making power  
- Approves corrective actions  
- Anchors findings to DAO ledger  

### 2. Oversight & Accountability Division  
- Conducts audits  
- Reviews misconduct  
- Issues findings and recommendations  

### 3. Operational Command  
- Implements corrective actions  
- Ensures SOP compliance  
- Provides operational logs  

### 4. Intelligence & Documentation Unit  
- Validates evidence  
- Maintains case files  
- Supports investigations  

### 5. Wellbeing & Civil Protections Unit  
- Ensures rights and trauma‑prevention  
- Reviews wellbeing impacts  
- Flags civil rights concerns  

See: **[Governance Structure](ca://s?q=Describe_governance_structure)**

## IV. Accountability Processes
The MTF uses a structured, auditable process for all reviews:

### A. Detection  
Issues may be identified through:  
- Telemetry anomalies  
- Evidence inconsistencies  
- Field reports  
- Community feedback  
- Rights or wellbeing concerns  

See: **[Telemetry Config](ca://s?q=Explain_telemetry_config)**

### B. Verification  
Oversight verifies:  
- Accuracy of documentation  
- Chain‑of‑custody integrity  
- Compliance with doctrine  
- Wellbeing and rights impacts  

See: **[Evidence Handling](ca://s?q=Explain_evidence_handling)**

### C. Classification  
Issues are classified as:  
- Documentation Error  
- Procedural Deviation  
- Ethical Concern  
- Rights Impact  
- Systemic Risk  

### D. Corrective Action  
Corrective actions may include:  
- Revision of documentation  
- SOP updates  
- Additional training  
- Escalation to Executive Authority  
- DAO‑anchored corrective directive  

### E. Closure  
A case is closed only when:  
- All corrections are complete  
- Oversight verifies compliance  
- DAO ledger entry is finalized

## V. Misconduct Review Protocol
Misconduct is defined as any action that violates:  
- Ethical standards  
- Operational doctrine  
- Civil rights protections  
- Documentation integrity  
- DAO authorship requirements  

Misconduct reviews must be:  
- Neutral  
- Evidence‑based  
- Trauma‑informed  
- Fully documented  

See: **[Civil Rights Protections](ca://s?q=Describe_civil_rights_protections)**

## VII. Corrective Action Hierarchy
Corrective actions follow a structured hierarchy:

1. **Informational Correction** — Minor documentation or procedural fix  
2. **Operational Correction** — SOP or workflow adjustment  
3. **Ethical Correction** — Conduct review and retraining  
4. **Rights Correction** — Civil protections intervention  
5. **Systemic Correction** — Structural or governance update  
6. **Executive Directive** — DAO‑anchored sovereign correction
