## I. Purpose
The Identity Protection Doctrine establishes the **sovereign safeguards**, **legal boundaries**, and **operational constraints** that govern the handling, verification, anchoring, and preservation of identity within Beast System 3.0.  
Identity is treated as a **sacred, sovereign, non‑transferable construct**, protected by constitutional law, DAO authorship, and integrity‑chain enforcement.

See: **[System Identity](ca://s?q=Describe_system_identity)**

---

## II. Identity Protection Principles
All identity operations must adhere to the following principles:

1. **Sovereignty** — Identity belongs solely to the individual or system entity.  
2. **Non‑Coercion** — Identity may never be extracted, demanded, or manipulated.  
3. **Integrity** — Identity must be hash‑verified and lineage‑anchored.  
4. **Minimalism** — Only essential identity attributes may be collected.  
5. **Confidentiality** — Identity data must be shielded from unauthorized access.  
6. **Rights Priority** — Identity protection supersedes operational convenience.  
7. **DAO Anchoring** — All identity artifacts must be anchored to the ledger.

See: **[Civil Rights Protections](ca://s?q=Describe_civil_rights_protections)**

## IV. Identity Protection Mechanisms

### A. Hash‑Based Integrity Protection
All identity artifacts must include:
- SHA‑256 or Beast Integrity Hash  
- Lineage hash  
- DAO anchor reference  

### B. Access Control Enforcement
Identity data may only be accessed by:
- Identity Layer  
- Registry Layer  
- Oversight Division (read‑only)  
- Executive Authority (escalations only)  

### C. Non‑Coercive Verification
Identity verification must:
- Be voluntary  
- Use non‑intrusive methods  
- Avoid forced disclosure  
- Respect personal boundaries  

See: **[Safety Protocols](ca://s?q=Describe_safety_protocols)**

### D. Immutable Lineage Protection
Identity artifacts must maintain:
- Continuous lineage  
- Immutable version history  
- DAO‑anchored authorship
