## I. Purpose
The Victim Support Protocols establish the **sovereign, trauma‑preventive, non‑coercive** standards that govern all interactions with individuals who have experienced harm, instability, discrimination, municipal misconduct, or rights‑impact events.  
These protocols ensure that every engagement prioritizes **safety, dignity, autonomy, and wellbeing**.

See: **[Wellbeing Framework](ca://s?q=Describe_wellbeing_framework)**

---

## II. Foundational Principles
All victim‑support actions must follow these principles:

1. **Dignity First** — Every person is treated with inherent worth.  
2. **Non‑Coercion** — No pressure, force, or implied obligation.  
3. **Trauma‑Prevention** — Interactions must avoid re‑triggering harm.  
4. **Safety Priority** — Emotional and physical safety override all other concerns.  
5. **Rights Protection** — All rights‑impact indicators trigger escalation.  
6. **Transparency** — Individuals understand what is documented about them.  
7. **Voluntary Participation** — Support is offered, never imposed.

See: **[Civil Rights Protections](ca://s?q=Describe_civil_rights_protections)**

---

## III. Victim Categories
The MTF recognizes **five sovereign victim categories**, each requiring tailored support:

1. **Direct Victims** — Individuals harmed by municipal actions or conditions.  
2. **Indirect Victims** — Family, dependents, or community members affected by harm.  
3. **Rights‑Impact Victims** — Individuals experiencing discrimination, coercion, or due‑process violations.  
4. **Wellbeing‑Impact Victims** — Individuals experiencing emotional or physical instability.  
5. **Systemic Victims** — Individuals harmed by repeated municipal failures or systemic neglect.

See: **[Risk Assessment](ca://s?q=Describe_risk_assessment)**

## IV. Engagement Protocols

### A. Initial Contact
Agents must:
- Approach calmly and respectfully  
- Maintain non‑threatening posture  
- Use neutral, supportive language  
- Avoid assumptions or interpretations  
- Allow the individual to set the pace  

### B. Communication Standards
- Speak slowly and clearly  
- Avoid leading questions  
- Avoid judgmental language  
- Validate feelings without exaggeration  
- Offer choices whenever possible  

### C. Consent Protocol
Before any documentation:
- Explain purpose  
- Explain rights  
- Explain voluntary nature  
- Ask for permission  
- Respect refusal  

See: **[Safety Protocols](ca://s?q=Describe_safety_protocols)**

---

## V. Support Actions

### A. Emotional Support
- Grounding techniques  
- Calm reassurance  
- Validation of experience  
- Offering space and time  

### B. Physical Safety Support
- Assess immediate danger  
- Relocate to safe environment  
- Document unsafe conditions  
- Trigger wellbeing escalation if needed  

### C. Rights‑Protection Support
- Document rights‑impact indicators  
- Notify Oversight Division  
- Provide clear explanation of next steps  
- Ensure no retaliation occurs  

See: **[Incident Escalation](ca://s?q=Explain_incident_escalation)**

### D. Resource Support
- Connect to community resources  
- Provide non‑punitive assistance  
- Offer stabilization pathways  
- Ensure follow‑up options  

---

## VI. Documentation Protocols

### A. Documentation Standards
All documentation must be:
- Neutral  
- Factual  
- Non‑interpretive  
- Timestamped  
- Hash‑verified  
- DAO‑anchored  

### B. Required Fields
- Victim category  
- Wellbeing indicators  
- Rights‑impact indicators  
- Environmental conditions  
- Direct quotes (if consented)  
- Support actions taken  

See: **[Case File Template](ca://s?q=Generate_case_file_template)**

---

## VII. Trauma‑Prevention Protocols

### A. Environmental Controls
- Reduce noise  
- Reduce crowding  
- Maintain personal space  
- Avoid sudden movements  

### B. Interaction Controls
- Avoid rapid questioning  
- Avoid confrontational tone  
- Avoid authoritative posture  
- Avoid unnecessary documentation in front of the victim  

### C. Emotional Controls
- Monitor distress indicators  
- Pause engagement if needed  
- Offer grounding or breaks  
- Avoid re‑exposure to traumatic details  

See: **[Wellbeing Framework](ca://s?q=Describe_wellbeing_framework)**

## VIII. Escalation Protocols

### A. Automatic Escalation Triggers
- Rights‑impact indicators  
- Severe emotional distress  
- Physical danger  
- Coercive municipal behavior  
- Discrimination indicators  
- Systemic risk patterns  

### B. Escalation Levels
- **L1** — Support  
- **L2** — Rights‑Impact  
- **L3** — Misconduct  
- **L4** — Systemic Risk  
- **L5** — Federal Escalation  

### C. Required Actions
- Notify Oversight Division  
- Document escalation rationale  
- Anchor escalation to DAO ledger  

See: **[Reporting Workflows](ca://s?q=Explain_reporting_workflows)**

## IX. Prohibited Actions
The following actions are strictly forbidden:

- Coercion  
- Threats  
- Retaliation  
- Surveillance  
- Forced disclosure  
- Punitive behavior  
- Discriminatory treatment  
- Pressuring victims to recount trauma  
- Misrepresenting documentation purpose  

These prohibitions are structural and cannot be overridden.
